Sources & attribution

Official station numbers: Estonian Environment Agency / Keskkonnaagentuur and Finnish Meteorological Institute (FMI, CC BY 4.0). A shared snapshot is refreshed every 10 minutes and checked every 5 minutes while enabled. Estonia reports the latest mean wind and gust with a feed timestamp; FMI supplies 10-minute mean wind and gust measurements sampled at hourly observation times. Historical selections use only earlier available readings; observations older than 3 hours are hidden. Station numbers are measured. Rain

Radar & rainfall accumulation

Sources & attribution

Nordic radar: FMI (Finland), SMHI (Sweden), MET Norway, DMI (Denmark), and the Icelandic Meteorological Office. Official data are decoded and reprojected to this map; source times are shown below. Reflectivity is converted to approximate rain intensity using a Z–R relation; snow, terrain and ground echoes can affect it. Iceland uses each available radar’s lowest corrected reflectivity scan, rather than a national composite. Missing observations stay transparent. Iceland is checked every minute while visible, through a cached live service reading official IMO scans. Polar cells are interpolated in linear reflectivity to soften rendering streaks; unknown observations remain transparent. Source terrain shadows and beam limitations can remain. A GitHub archive is retained as fallback. Denmark reads official DMI observation times directly and displays DMI’s official web radar images, reprojected to this map. Grey source cartography is masked. The GitHub archive remains a fallback if direct metadata is unavailable.

Iceland uses published 1-, 24- and 48-hour station totals; Latvia and Norway use official hourly measurements; Norway includes MET Norway stations with measured primary-sensor rainfall. Open-Meteo rain + showers (CC BY 4.0) fills unsupported countries and gaps. Coastlines: Natural Earth / world-atlas.

Cyclones

Centres, history & forecasts

Sources & attribution

NOAA / NCEP GFS model fields for low-pressure centres, pressure, movement, wind, gusts and tracks. Model-derived cold and warm fronts use the same GFS cycle with 850 hPa temperature and wind; they are an objective diagnostic rather than an official hand-analysed synoptic chart. NOAA NHC supplies matched Atlantic tropical-storm names. European names are checked against dated Met Office storm impacts and named MET Norway wind alerts. Matches to model centres are inferred from region and time; ambiguous systems keep tracking IDs. Past tracks contain elapsed modelled positions, not official observed tracks.

NOAA GFS ↗NOAA NHC ↗
Solid trails show elapsed modelled centres, retained across matched model runs as history accumulates. Dashed paths show the forecast with 12-hour steps. Click a rotating centre for pressure, movement, nearby wind and gusts. Use View coverage to find systems across the region.
Closed low-pressure centres and paths derived from NOAA GFS; colours indicate pressure. Only centres at or below 1000 hPa are displayed. Includes ordinary Atlantic and European lows. Isobars use the same GFS sea-level pressure forecast, with 1 hPa spacing at every zoom. Pressure trends show 6-hour deepening or filling; this is not an impact warning. Official Atlantic tropical-storm names are shown when matched to NHC; other lows use tracking IDs. Forecast time is independent of the rain-radar timeline. New model runs every 6 hours; feed checked about every hour, allowing 4 hours for a complete run. Centres and isobars use matching hourly forecast frames. Now advances automatically to the latest completed hour; playback advances one hour at a time. “Older model run” means the run is over 12 hours old. All times are local to your device.
Track spread uses NOAA GEFS control and 30 perturbed forecasts from the same model cycle as GFS, sampled every 6 hours. Newly published cycles extend as later forecast frames arrive; only available hours are drawn. Each member low is matched one-to-one by trajectory and pressure; ambiguous matches are omitted. Pale blue shading shows the 80th-percentile radial distance around median matched centres, only where at least 10 and half of available members support the track. This is descriptive ensemble spread, not an 80% probability cone, a warning or a wind-impact area. The optional thin blue lines show individual matched member tracks. The existing GFS path stays unchanged; it is a single model forecast, not an ensemble probability maximum.

Sources & attribution

Estonian Environment Agency, LVĢMC, LHMT / Meteo.lt, FMI CAP, IMGW–PIB, DMI, and MeteoAlarm / EUMETNET national services for the other supported Nordic countries. Country outlines: Natural Earth / world-atlas. Estonian marine warning zones follow Natural Earth coastlines and exclude mainland and mapped islands; Lake Peipsi follows the lake outline. Their outer forecast-zone boundaries approximate the official named regions.

MeteoAlarm ↗Estonia ↗LVĢMC ↗LHMT ↗FMI ↗IMGW ↗DMI ↗
Shows unexpired warnings affecting today or tomorrow in each country’s local time. Estonia loads the complete official county and marine forecast about every 15 minutes and uses the exact warning start and end times; upcoming warnings are labelled “starts tomorrow”. Poland uses official IMGW county polygons; Denmark uses official DMI municipality and coastal polygons. Their feeds are copied about every 15 minutes; expired polygons disappear automatically. Snow

Daily snow cover & sea ice

Sources & attribution

NOAA / U.S. National Ice Center / National Weather Service. Interactive Multisensor Snow and Ice Mapping System (IMS), daily Northern Hemisphere snow and ice analysis at 1 km resolution. Analysts combine satellite and surface information.

USNIC IMS ↗NOAA map service ↗Dataset & citation ↗

Latest maps use the 1 km NOAA service. The rolling 14-day daily history uses official 4 km IMS analyses from the NSIDC archive. Dates are the valid 00:00 UTC analysis dates; missing days are omitted, never interpolated. Station depths are hidden on historical dates because they are current observations. White shows snow-covered land, cyan shows sea ice. Transparent areas include snow-free surfaces and missing data. The white/cyan imagery shows coverage, not depth or falling snow. Station labels provide measured snow depths separately. Depth sources: Keskkonnaagentuur daily climate data (cm), FMI daily snow observations (cm), SMHI parameter 8 (metres converted to cm), LVĢMC hourly station measurements (cm, CC0), LHMT / Meteo.lt snowDepth (cm, CC BY-SA 4.0), MET Norway / Seklima surface_snow_thickness (cm, CC BY 3.0 NO), and Icelandic Meteorological Office daily 09:00 UTC snow depths (cm). LHMT data are adapted into the shared snapshot under CC BY-SA 4.0; source readings retain their dates. Norway includes MET Norway stations and excludes modelled, interpolated and erroneous values. Icelandic measurements are provisional. Special trace and patchy snow codes remain separate from numeric depths. Provisional readings are identified in the popup. The map projection displays up to 85°N; the immediate polar cap is outside this view.
